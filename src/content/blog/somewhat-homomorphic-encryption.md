## Notation and Preliminaries

Let $q \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ be the ciphertext modulus, and define $\mathbb{Z}_q = \{0, 1, \dots, q-1\}$. For any integer $a$, we denote its reduction modulo $q$ as $[a]_q$, i.e., the remainder of $a \mod q$. For example, $[18]_7 = 4$. We use the rounding operator $\lfloor \cdot \rceil$ to denote rounding to the nearest integer.

Let $n$ be a power of two. Define:

* The **plaintext modulus** $t \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$,
* The **ciphertext modulus** $q \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$,
* The **scaling factor** $\Delta = \left\lfloor \frac{q}{t} \right\rfloor$.

Let:

* $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^n + 1)$ be the ring of integer polynomials modulo the cyclotomic polynomial $x^n + 1$,
* $R_t = \mathbb{Z}_t[x]/(x^n + 1)$ be the plaintext ring,
* $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^n + 1)$ be the ciphertext ring.

We also define:

* $\chi$: a discrete error distribution over $R$, typically a variant of a discrete Gaussian,
* $\| \cdot \|$: the maximum absolute coefficient value in a polynomial.

From this point on, all polynomial arithmetic is implicitly carried out modulo $x^n + 1$.

---

## Key Generation

* **Secret Key** $sk = s \in R$ is a binary polynomial with coefficients in $\{0, 1\}$.
* **Public Key** is generated by:

  1. Sampling $a \leftarrow R_q$ uniformly,
  2. Sampling $e \leftarrow \chi$,
  3. Computing:

     $$
     pk = ([-(a \cdot s + e)]_q, \; a) \in R_q \times R_q.
     $$

The hardness of the **Ring Learning With Errors (RLWE)** assumption ensures that it is computationally infeasible to recover $s$ from $pk$.

---

## Encryption

To encrypt a plaintext message $m \in R_t$, do the following:

1. Let $pk = (pk_0, pk_1)$,
2. Sample $u, e_1, e_2 \leftarrow \chi$,
3. Compute the ciphertext:

   $$
   \textsf{Enc}(pk, m) = \left( [pk_0 \cdot u + e_1 + \Delta \cdot m]_q,\; [pk_1 \cdot u + e_2]_q \right) \in R_q \times R_q.
   $$

Because of the noise terms and the RLWE assumption, the ciphertext is statistically indistinguishable from random and thus reveals no information about $m$.

In implementation, the message $m$ is first represented as an integer vector of length $\leq n$ with entries in $\{0, \dots, t-1\}$, then padded and mapped into $R_t$.

---

## Decryption

Let $ct = (ct_0, ct_1) = \textsf{Enc}(pk, m)$. The decryption algorithm uses the secret key $s$ as follows:

$$
\textsf{Dec}(sk, ct) = \left[ \left\lfloor \frac{t}{q} \cdot [ct_0 + ct_1 \cdot s]_q \right\rceil \right]_t \in R_t.
$$

### Decryption Intuition and Correctness

Let us define:

$$
ct(s) := ct_0 + ct_1 \cdot s.
$$

Substituting the encryption and key generation expressions into $ct(s)$, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[ct(s)]_q &= [pk_0 \cdot u + e_1 + \Delta \cdot m + pk_1 \cdot u \cdot s + e_2 \cdot s]_q \\
&= [(-a \cdot s - e) \cdot u + e_1 + \Delta \cdot m + a \cdot u \cdot s + e_2 \cdot s]_q \\
&= [\Delta \cdot m + e_1 - e \cdot u + e_2 \cdot s]_q \\
&= \Delta \cdot m + v,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $v = e_1 - e \cdot u + e_2 \cdot s$ is the **aggregate noise term**.

As long as the noise $\|v\| < \Delta / 2$, rounding will yield the correct plaintext:

$$
\left\lfloor \frac{t}{q} \cdot (\Delta \cdot m + v) \right\rceil = m.
$$

Thus, correctness is guaranteed as long as the noise growth is controlled appropriately throughout homomorphic operations.
