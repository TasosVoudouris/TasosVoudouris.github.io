## Ross-course technical reconstruction

### Gaussian reduction

The old notebook used the basis `(104,62)`, `(74,23)`. The canonical companion now implements exact nearest-integer Gauss reduction in dependency-free Python.

### One-dimensional NTRU analogy

The source uses:

- `q = 122430513841`;
- `f = 231231`;
- `g = 195698`;
- `h = f^{-1} g mod q`.

The public lattice generated by `(1,h)` and `(0,q)` contains `(f,g)`. Exact 2D Gaussian reduction recovers `±(f,g)` from public data. The canonical article adds the correctness/no-wrap condition that the original notebook leaves implicit.

### Polynomial NTRU / LLL

For the recovered `N=7, p=3, q=41` toy, the canonical article reconstructs the circulant public-key matrix and the block lattice

```text
[ I   H ]
[ 0  qI ]
```

and proves that `(f,g)` belongs to it. The dependency-free LLL companion recovers the same short equivalent key exhibited by the Sage notebook and verifies the `-x^4` rotation that maps it back to the original secret pair.

The article explicitly avoids the misleading statement "LLL breaks NTRU": the demonstration succeeds because the dimension and parameters are deliberately tiny.

## Random-Sage technical corrections

The historical Sage worksheets contain useful examples but also several bugs or misleading labels. The canonical versions correct them rather than preserving them as executable truth.

### Classical material

- A worksheet labels monoalphabetic **substitution** as "transposition"; the canonical article distinguishes the two.
- Double affine encryption is shown correctly to collapse to one affine map:
  `a3 = a2*a1 mod 26`, `b3 = a2*b1+b2 mod 26`.
- For `(a1,b1)=(3,5)` and `(a2,b2)=(11,7)`, the correct composed key is `(7,10)`, not the worksheet's accidental `(20,10)` test value.
- Affine/Hill inverses are conditioned on invertibility in `Z_26`; nonzero is not enough over a composite modulus.
- Sage's `^` exponent syntax is not portable to ordinary Python, where it means XOR.

### Public-key material

- The old RSA prime generator does not guarantee the requested modulus bit length and does not explicitly reject `p=q`.
- Raw RSA exponentiation is labeled textbook arithmetic, not secure OAEP encryption.
- The CRT reconstruction algebra is retained and tested.
- The common-modulus attack is kept with explicit handling of negative Bézout exponents via modular inverses.
- The Diffie–Hellman parameter function accepts a `bits` argument but effectively ignores it by sampling tiny hard-coded primes; this is corrected in the explanation and not copied into the canonical code.
- The old "ElGamal" exercise is actually a DH-derived multiplicative mask, not textbook ElGamal. The cleaned lab uses the real ciphertext pair `(g^k, m*y^k)`.
- ECDH's group equation is kept while the article adds public-key validation, subgroup, randomness, serialization, and KDF boundaries.
- The textbook RSA signature existential-forgery exercise is retained as a valid demonstration of why raw RSA is not a secure signature scheme.
