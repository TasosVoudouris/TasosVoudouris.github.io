# RSA Deep Dive XII — ROCA Cross-Check

## Scope

This package is site-first and scientific.

The companion implements only a fixed toy **fingerprint/model-check**.

It does not include:

- public-key harvesting;
- certificate scanning;
- generic ROCA detection across datasets;
- Coppersmith factorization of real or arbitrary RSA moduli;
- target selection.

The factorization phase is described only at the mathematical architecture level.

## Primary ROCA paper

Verified primary source:

```text
Matúš Nemec
Marek Sýs
Petr Švenda
Dušan Klinec
Vashek Matyáš

The Return of Coppersmith's Attack:
Practical Factorization of Widely Used RSA Moduli

ACM CCS 2017
pp. 1631-1648
DOI 10.1145/3133956.3133969
```

The paper states that the vulnerable generated primes have the form:

```text
p = k*M + (65537^a mod M)
```

where `M` is a known primorial.

It also states that the weakness:

```text
does not depend on a weak or faulty RNG.
```

The issue is the structured prime-generation family itself.

---

## Public fingerprint derivation

If:

```text
p = k*M + g^a mod M
q = l*M + g^b mod M
g = 65537
```

then:

```text
p = g^a mod M
q = g^b mod M
```

and therefore:

```text
N = p*q
  = g^(a+b) mod M.
```

Thus:

```text
N mod M in <g>.
```

The paper identifies the existence of the discrete logarithm:

```text
log_g(N mod M)
```

inside the subgroup generated by `g` as the public fingerprint.

The subgroup order is smooth enough that the original work uses
Pohlig-Hellman efficiently.

## Real-paper entropy example

For the 512-bit RSA case, the paper reports approximately:

```text
prime p size  = 256 bits
M size        = 219 bits
k size        = 37 bits
a size        = 62 bits
```

giving approximately:

```text
37 + 62 = 99 bits
```

of construction entropy instead of a generic 256-bit prime search space.

The article presents this only as a historical example from the paper.

## Factorization wording

The article does not reduce ROCA to:

```text
fingerprint => factors immediately.
```

It distinguishes:

```text
fingerprinting
```

from:

```text
factor reconstruction.
```

The paper explains that a naive enumeration of the exponent `a` followed by
Coppersmith is still too expensive.

The researchers instead use an alternative representation with a smaller
divisor `M'` of the primorial, reducing the guess space while leaving enough
known modular structure for a Coppersmith/Howgrave-Graham factor-recovery
step.

The public article describes that architecture without implementing the full
factorization method.

## Scientific taxonomy

The article distinguishes ROCA from:

### weak RNG / shared-prime failures

```text
bad entropy source
-> repeated/predictable candidates
```

### ROCA

```text
random choices inside a restricted algebraic family
-> structurally biased primes
-> public subgroup fingerprint
```

Thus:

```text
good randomness inside a bad sample space
```

can still create weak RSA keys.

## Publication checklist

Verify:

- Is ROCA introduced as key-generation structure, not generic RSA failure?
- Is the exact prime form stated?
- Is `M` defined as a primorial?
- Is the subgroup `<65537>` defined?
- Is `N mod M = 65537^(a+b) mod M` derived?
- Is fingerprinting separated from factorization?
- Is subgroup order/smoothness connected to Pohlig-Hellman?
- Is the toy subgroup fully reproducible?
- Are both toy primes verified prime?
- Is the structured modulus fingerprint checked?
- Is a comparison modulus shown outside the subgroup?
- Is entropy loss explained structurally?
- Is weak RNG explicitly distinguished from structured prime generation?
- Is the Coppersmith connection described but not duplicated?
- Is the "large modulus != quality key" lesson explicit?
- Does the article naturally lead to an RSA synthesis post?

Any "no" means another revision.
